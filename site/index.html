  • You take a 25-question quiz that lasts 10–15 minutes
  • We'll match you with federal occupations that hire at entry level, based on your interests
  • You see how much each job hires, what's open now, and what you need to qualify

Not affiliated with USAJOBS or the U.S. Office of Personnel Management. This is an independent rebuild of the government's Career Explorer, using its published hiring records and job postings. The official tool is at usajobs.gov/careerexplorer.

How this works

Every one of the 302 occupations carries a rating from 0 to 4 on each question: 0 means that kind of work is not part of the job, 4 means it is the core of it. Your answers become 25 numbers too, and occupations are ranked by how closely the two sets move together. You can see the ratings behind any result on its card.

The ratings are a language model's reading of each occupation, based on real job postings rather than job titles. They are a judgement, not a measurement, and the thing that gets tested is whether the questions tell occupations apart — never whether the ratings are right.

The questions are written and then measured the same way. A question everything scores the same on is useless, and two occupations rated alike can never be separated by any answer you give. The official 32 questions leave the biggest entry-level hirers at 0.17 average similarity to each other; these 25 get that to 0.07.

Everything else on a card comes from federal records: hires are OPM personnel data from 2021 on, averaged per year; openings are current USAJOBS postings, counting only those open to the public, students or recent graduates; the education requirement combines posting text, OPM's published standard, and what people hired into the job actually held.
